Language map sources

Solid outlines show established language communities and widespread second-language use. Administrative regions are approximate geographic references. Very small countries may use a point marker at this scale.

This is a curated overview, not an exhaustive map of speakers, a population-density map, or a claim that everyone within a boundary speaks the language. Administrative areas approximate linguistic regions. Smaller communities and diaspora populations are incompletely represented. Related languages and creoles are not automatically counted as the same language. Arabic is shown as a language grouping including its regional varieties.

Country boundaries: World Atlas / Natural Earth (public domain). Regional boundaries: geoBoundaries gbOpen, with original providers and licences in its country metadata. Indian boundaries: DataMeet / Election Commission of India, CC BY 2.5 India. Other regional datasets retain their source-specific attribution licences. French Basque boundary: API Découpage administratif. Location maps: US Census Bureau TIGERweb.
